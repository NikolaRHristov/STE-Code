## Rule 8.7 Hyphenated words count as one word.
Groups of words that are not usually adjectives but function together as an adjective before a noun
are hyphenated. Such groups of words count as one word.
Examples in STE:
Clean the surface with a soap-and-water solution. (7 words)
Use the trial-and-error method. (4 words)
When you hyphenate long technical names to make them clearer to the reader, a hyphenated
group of words also counts as one word in the applicable noun cluster.
Examples in STE:
Cutoff-switch power connection (3 words)
Main-gear-door retraction-winch handle (3 words)
